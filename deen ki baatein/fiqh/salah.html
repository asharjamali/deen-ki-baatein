Salah - Islamic Prayer

Learn the rulings and steps for performing the five daily prayers

About Salah

Salah, the second pillar of Islam, is the obligatory five daily prayers performed by Muslims to maintain a direct connection with Allah. It is a physical, mental, and spiritual act of worship performed at specific times: Fajr, Dhuhr, Asr, Maghrib, and Isha.

Conditions of Salah

Purity (Wudu or Ghusl), proper timing, facing the Qibla, covering the Awrah, and intention (Niyyah) are mandatory for a valid Salah.

Steps of Salah

Includes Takbir, Qiyam, Ruku, Sujud, Tashahhud, and Salam, performed in a specific sequence for each Rak’ah.

Prayer Times

Fajr (dawn), Dhuhr (midday), Asr (afternoon), Maghrib (sunset), Isha (night). Times vary based on location.

Key Ruling

Facing the Qibla
Obligation to face the Ka’bah during Salah
Reference: Surah Al-Baqarah [2:144]

Muslims must face the Qibla (direction of the Ka’bah in Makkah) during Salah, as commanded in the Quran. If unsure of the direction, one may rely on reasonable estimation or tools like a compass.
